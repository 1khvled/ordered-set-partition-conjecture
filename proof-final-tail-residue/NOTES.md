# Older-tail residue domination: RETRACTION

> **RETRACTED — the alleged counterexample was caused by a derivative-indexing bug.**
> The old script evaluated `x*q'(x)` instead of `q'(x)`. The exact rational
> certificate `certify_wrs_failure.py` isolates the root by Sturm and proves
> that `O_63/q'` is negative there, so WRS holds at that root. No actual-family
> WRS counterexample has been established. The text below is retained only as
> an audit trail and must not be cited.

Date: 2026-11-27  
Scope: only `ordered-big-blocks/proof-final-tail-residue`.

## Status

**Obstruction found.** The older-tail residue domination inequality

\[
\sum_{r=1}^{j-1}\binom nr\Bigl(-\frac{O_{n-r}(\mu)}{q'(\mu)}\Bigr)
\;>\;
\mu\sum_{r=j+1}^{n}\binom nr\frac{O_{n-r}(\mu)}{q'(\mu)},
\qquad q=O_{n-j},\;q(\mu)=0,
\tag{10}
\]

does **not** hold for every actual root \(\mu\). A high-precision counterexample is given below. Consequently (10) cannot be used as an all-index proof of WRS, and the WRS route through the first-block decomposition fails at this point.

## 1. What was tested

For fixed \(j,n\), the coefficients \(O_m\) were generated by the exact first-block recurrence

\[
O_n=\sum_{r=1}^{j-1}\binom nr O_{n-r}
+t\sum_{r=j}^{n}\binom nr O_{n-r},\qquad O_0=1,
\]

and every real root \(\mu\) of \(q=O_{n-j}\) was evaluated at 200-digit precision. The margin

\[
M(\mu)=\sum_{r=1}^{j-1}\binom nr\Bigl(-\frac{O_{n-r}(\mu)}{q'(\mu)}\Bigr)
-\mu\sum_{r=j+1}^{n}\binom nr\frac{O_{n-r}(\mu)}{q'(\mu)}
\]

was computed. \(M(\mu)>0\) is exactly (10), equivalently \(O_n(\mu)/q'(\mu)<0\).

- For \(j=3\), \(n\le 60\): no negative margins (after filtering numerical roots).
- For \(j=4\), \(n\le 40\): no negative margins.
- For \(j=2\), \(n\le 80\): negative margins occur.

## 2. Counterexample

Take

\[
j=2,\qquad n=63,\qquad q=O_{61}.
\]

Then \(\deg q=30\). One real root is

\[
\mu=-0.30104986453933631902077111559346277472551444946832328224863334196710538985550720874877335783698920026924780788474743635899832978336347134018484334053769805929409775181140824704538989943190668464507612\ldots
\]

with \(q(\mu)\approx -9.35\times 10^{-115}\) and

\[
q'(\mu)\approx -2.678263360972212\ldots\times 10^{?}
\]

nonzero. At this root,

\[
\frac{O_{63}(\mu)}{q'(\mu)}
= 16.516697881992691\ldots >0.
\]

Since

\[
\frac{O_n(\mu)}{q'(\mu)}
= -\sum_{r=1}^{j-1}\binom nr\frac{O_{n-r}(\mu)}{q'(\mu)}
+\mu\sum_{r=j+1}^{n}\binom nr\frac{O_{n-r}(\mu)}{q'(\mu)},
\]

this gives

\[
M(\mu)=-16.516697881992691\ldots <0.
\]

Thus the recent negative part does **not** dominate the signed older tail at this root.

The computation was verified at 200-digit precision in `verify_bad2.py`; the residual \(q(\mu)\) is below \(10^{-114}\).

## 3. Consequence

The first-block decomposition and the compatibility window prove only that the recent sum is negative. They do not imply (10). The formal 200-digit counterexample shows that the older tail can (and, for \(j=2\), does) overcome the recent part at an actual root.

Therefore:

1. The inequality (10) is false as a universal statement.
2. WRS cannot be established from the first-block decomposition plus local compatibility.
3. Any all-index proof of real-rootedness must use a different invariant or a
   stronger, genuinely global bound on the older rows.
